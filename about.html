XOCHIMILCO DE MUERTOS Un xolo, una canoa y las almas perdidas

It's the night of Día de Muertos in Xochimilco, Mexico City. You're a xoloitzcuintle (the hairless dog who guides souls to the land of the dead) paddling a canoa through the canals. Lost souls are drifting on the water. Gather as many as you can and bring them to the ofrenda before dawn.

How a run works

  • Souls are your score. Each soul you touch joins the glowing trail behind your canoa. Souls floating up high need a jump.
  • Getting hit scatters half your trail. The scattered souls fly off and land back in the lanes, so you can win them back. You're then invulnerable for 1.5 seconds and flicker.
  • Your candle is your light. It burns down over about 45 seconds, and the fog closes in as it dims. Each candle you pick up refills half of it, and a candle shows up at least every 9 seconds.
  • Speed rises gently over the run, and the sky moves from purple midnight toward an orange dawn.
  • The finale: in the last zone the ofrenda rises out of the water. Your souls fly up to it one by one (ENTREGADAS) and you get the win screen. Points = souls delivered × 10 + petals.

The five zones

The thin bar at the top of the screen shows how far you are from the ofrenda, and the box beside it shows the zone you're in (e.g. ZONA 4/5 EL LAGO). A full run takes about three minutes, or a little over one with ?short.

1 · EMBARCADERO: the trajinera dock at Nativitas. A short tutorial teaches steering, jumping, lirios, ducking and candles. Boats are parked along the banks, and the first Lloronas appear.
2 · CANAL PRINCIPAL: the busiest stretch. Rows of trajineras, mariachi boats (listen for the trumpet), vendor canoes cutting across, bridges, and papel picado to duck under.
3 · ISLA DE LAS MUÑECAS: Island of the Dolls. Dolls hang from the trees, the fog is thick, and you get more souls but less light. La Llorona comes at you in waves and floats overhead.
4 · EL LAGO: the canal opens into a wide lake under a dawn-pink horizon. This is La Llorona's home, so expect many of her, sometimes in pairs or groups of three, plus drifting boats on the open water.
5 · LA OFRENDA: no obstacles. The marigold arch and ofrenda rise from the water, and you deliver your souls.

Demo mode & shortcuts

  • Very easy by design. Hitboxes are 60% the size of the sprites. Getting hit with an empty trail doesn't end the run; you just flicker. The game-over screen is still there behind CONFIG.allowGameOver.
  • 1–5 jump straight to a zone at any time, including from the title or win screen (numpad works too). N skips to the next zone. Esc goes back to the title.
  • Phone: tap with three fingers to skip to the next zone. The title screen has a button for each zone.
  • URL options: ?zone=4 starts at a zone, and ?short cuts every zone to 40% of its length. Combine them: ?zone=3&short.
  • When you skip ahead with an empty trail, you're given 8 souls so the finale still has something to deliver.

How it's made

  • Static site, frontend only. There's no build step, no backend and not a single image file. Source on GitHub.
  • 3D with Three.js (loaded from a CDN through an import map). The scene renders to a 640×360 target and is scaled up to the window with nearest-neighbor filtering and 16:9 letterboxing. The HUD is a 320×180 canvas drawn on top.
  • Every sprite is a grid of characters written in code (sprites.js) using a locked 16-color palette. Each one is doubled with the EPX pixel-art scaler, then given rim light, shadow and texture. La Llorona is drawn at full resolution by hand. All sprites are camera-facing billboards in a 3D canal.
  • All sound is synthesized live with the Web Audio API: paddle splashes, soul chimes, mariachi trumpet cues, La Llorona's wail and an ambient drone.
  • Every tuning value (speed, zone length, spawn mix, hitbox size, candle drain, number of Lloronas…) lives in one CONFIG object at the top of main.js.
